INTRODUCTION TO FORCE

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of Force

Force is a push or pull that can change the state of motion, direction, speed or shape of an object.

2. Effects of Force

A force can make a stationary object move, stop a moving object, change the speed of an object, change its direction of motion or change its shape.

3. SI Unit of Force

The SI unit of force is the newton (N). A force of one newton produces an acceleration of one metre per second squared in a mass of one kilogram.

1 N = 1 kg m/s²

4. Types of Forces

Forces can be broadly classified as contact forces and non-contact forces.

Contact forces:
Frictional force, muscular force, tension and normal reaction.

Non-contact forces:
Gravitational force, magnetic force and electrostatic force.

5. Frictional Force

Friction is a force that opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact. It can be useful, for example in walking and braking, but it can also cause wear and energy loss.

6. Gravitational Force

Gravitational force is the force of attraction between masses. The gravitational attraction of the Earth pulls objects towards its centre.

7. Magnetic Force

Magnetic force is a force associated with magnets. Magnets can attract magnetic materials such as iron and can also attract or repel other magnets.

8. Electrostatic Force

Electrostatic force is the force between electrically charged objects. Like charges repel each other while unlike charges attract each other.

9. Measuring Force

Force can be measured using a spring balance or Newton meter. The reading is usually given in newtons (N).

10. Force and Motion

When forces act on an object, they may change its motion. An unbalanced force can cause acceleration, deceleration or a change in direction.

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: What is force?

Force is a push or pull that can change the motion, direction, speed or shape of an object.

Example 2: State the SI unit of force.

The SI unit of force is the newton (N).

Example 3: A force of 20 N acts on a mass of 5 kg. Find the acceleration.

Using:

F = ma

a = F ÷ m
a = 20 ÷ 5
a = 4 m/s²

Example 4: Give two examples of non-contact forces.

Gravitational force and magnetic force.
