LINEAR MOTION

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of Motion

Motion is the change in position of an object with time relative to a reference point.

Example: A car moving along a road is in motion relative to a person standing beside the road.

2. Rest and Motion

An object is said to be at rest when its position does not change with time relative to a chosen reference point. An object is in motion when its position changes with time.

3. Distance

Distance is the total length of the path travelled by an object. Distance is a scalar quantity.

SI unit of distance = metre (m)

4. Displacement

Displacement is the shortest distance in a specified direction from the initial position to the final position. Displacement is a vector quantity.

Important: Distance has magnitude only, while displacement has both magnitude and direction.

5. Speed

Speed is the distance travelled per unit time.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time
v = d / t

The SI unit of speed is metre per second (m/s).

6. Average Speed

Average speed is the total distance travelled divided by the total time taken.

Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time

7. Velocity

Velocity is the displacement travelled per unit time in a specified direction. Velocity is a vector quantity.

Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time

8. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time.

Acceleration = Change in Velocity ÷ Time
a = (v - u) / t

where u is the initial velocity and v is the final velocity.

9. Deceleration

Deceleration is the rate at which velocity decreases with time. It is also called negative acceleration.

10. Uniform Motion

An object is in uniform motion when it travels equal distances in equal intervals of time.

11. Non-uniform Motion

An object is in non-uniform motion when it travels unequal distances in equal intervals of time, or when its direction changes.

12. Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as

where:

  • u = initial velocity
  • v = final velocity
  • a = acceleration
  • t = time
  • s = displacement

13. Distance-Time Graph

A distance-time graph shows how the distance travelled by an object changes with time. The gradient of a distance-time graph represents speed.

14. Velocity-Time Graph

A velocity-time graph shows how velocity changes with time. The gradient represents acceleration, while the area under the graph represents displacement.

15. Summary of Motion Quantities

Quantity Meaning SI Unit
Distance Total path travelled m
Displacement Shortest distance in a specified direction m
Speed Distance per unit time m/s
Velocity Displacement per unit time m/s
Acceleration Change in velocity per unit time m/s²

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: A car travels 120 m in 10 seconds. Calculate its speed.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Speed = 120 ÷ 10

Speed = 12 m/s

Example 2: A runner travels 400 m in 50 seconds. Find the average speed.

Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time

= 400 ÷ 50

= 8 m/s

Example 3: A car starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 seconds. Find its final velocity.

u = 0 m/s
a = 2 m/s²
t = 5 s

v = u + at

v = 0 + (2 × 5)

v = 10 m/s

Example 4: A car has an initial velocity of 5 m/s and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 seconds. Find its final velocity.

v = u + at

v = 5 + (3 × 4)

v = 17 m/s

Example 5: An object starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 4 seconds. Find the displacement.

u = 0
a = 2 m/s²
t = 4 s

s = ut + ½at²

s = (0 × 4) + ½(2)(4²)

s = 1 × 16

s = 16 m

Example 6: A vehicle changes its velocity from 10 m/s to 20 m/s in 5 seconds. Calculate its acceleration.

a = (v - u) ÷ t

a = (20 - 10) ÷ 5

a = 2 m/s²
