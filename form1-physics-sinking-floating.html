SINKING AND FLOATING

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of Sinking and Floating

When an object is placed in a liquid, it may sink, float or remain suspended depending on the relationship between its weight, upthrust and average density.

2. Upthrust

Upthrust is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an object that is partially or completely immersed in the fluid.

Upthrust = Weight of fluid displaced

3. Archimedes' Principle

Archimedes' Principle states that a body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid experiences an upthrust equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body.

4. Conditions for Floating

An object floats when the upward upthrust balances its weight. For an object floating freely, the weight of the object is equal to the upthrust.

Upthrust = Weight of object

5. Conditions for Sinking

An object sinks when its weight is greater than the upthrust acting on it. In general, an object sinks in a liquid when its average density is greater than the density of the liquid.

6. Suspended Objects

An object is suspended in a fluid when its weight is equal to the upthrust while it remains completely inside the fluid. Its average density is equal to the density of the fluid.

7. Law of Flotation

The law of flotation states that a floating body displaces a quantity of fluid whose weight is equal to the weight of the body.

Weight of floating body = Weight of fluid displaced

8. Factors Affecting Floating

Floating depends mainly on the density of the object relative to the density of the fluid, the object's shape and the amount of fluid displaced.

9. Why Ships Float

A ship is made of materials that may have a density greater than water, but its hollow shape contains air and gives the whole ship a low average density. The ship therefore displaces enough water to produce an upthrust equal to its weight.

10. Applications of Flotation

The principles of flotation are applied in ships, boats, submarines, hydrometers and life-saving devices.

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: A floating object weighs 500 N. What is the upthrust acting on it?

For a floating object:

Upthrust = Weight of object

Upthrust = 500 N

Example 2: An object displaces water weighing 80 N. According to Archimedes' Principle, find the upthrust.

Upthrust = Weight of fluid displaced

Upthrust = 80 N

Example 3: An object has an average density of 700 kg/m³ and is placed in water of density 1000 kg/m³. Will it float or sink?

Density of object < Density of water

700 < 1000

The object will float.

Example 4: An object has an average density of 1200 kg/m³ and is placed in water of density 1000 kg/m³. Will it float or sink?

Density of object > Density of water

1200 > 1000

The object will sink.
