WORK, ENERGY AND POWER

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of Work

In Physics, work is done when a force causes an object to move through a distance in the direction of the force.

Work = Force × Distance
W = F × d

The SI unit of work is the joule (J).

2. Conditions Necessary for Work to be Done

For work to be done in Physics:

  • A force must act on an object.
  • The object must move through a distance.
  • The movement must have a component in the direction of the applied force.
Important: If there is no movement, mechanical work done is zero, even when a force is being applied.

3. Energy

Energy is the capacity or ability to do work.

SI unit of energy = joule (J)

4. Forms of Energy

Energy exists in different forms, including:

  • Kinetic energy
  • Potential energy
  • Heat energy
  • Light energy
  • Sound energy
  • Electrical energy
  • Chemical energy

5. Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object because of its motion.

K.E = ½mv²

where m is mass and v is velocity.

6. Potential Energy

Potential energy is stored energy possessed by an object because of its position or condition.

Gravitational potential energy is given by:

P.E = mgh

where m is mass, g is gravitational acceleration and h is height.

7. Mechanical Energy

Mechanical energy is the total of kinetic energy and potential energy.

Mechanical Energy = K.E + P.E

8. Conservation of Energy

The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another.

Example: In a falling object, gravitational potential energy is gradually transformed into kinetic energy.

9. Power

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

Power = Work ÷ Time
P = W / t

The SI unit of power is the watt (W).

10. Relationship Between Power and Energy

Since work is a form of energy transfer, power can also be expressed as the rate of energy transfer.

Power = Energy ÷ Time

11. Efficiency

Efficiency is the ratio of useful output energy or work to the total input energy or work, usually expressed as a percentage.

Efficiency = Useful Output ÷ Total Input × 100%

12. Simple Machines and Mechanical Advantage

Simple machines make work easier by changing the size or direction of a force. Examples include levers, pulleys, inclined planes, wheel and axle, screws and wedges.

Mechanical Advantage = Load ÷ Effort

13. Summary of Quantities

Quantity Formula SI Unit
Work W = Fd J
Kinetic Energy K.E = ½mv² J
Potential Energy P.E = mgh J
Power P = W/t W
Mechanical Advantage MA = Load/Effort No unit
Efficiency Useful output/Input × 100% %

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: A force of 20 N moves an object through 5 m. Calculate the work done.

W = F × d

W = 20 × 5

W = 100 J

Example 2: A 4 kg object moves at 3 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy.

K.E = ½mv²

= ½ × 4 × 3²

= 2 × 9

= 18 J

Example 3: A 2 kg object is raised to a height of 5 m. Take g = 10 m/s². Calculate its potential energy.

P.E = mgh

= 2 × 10 × 5

= 100 J

Example 4: A machine does 600 J of work in 20 seconds. Calculate its power.

P = W ÷ t

P = 600 ÷ 20

P = 30 W

Example 5: A machine has a load of 400 N and an effort of 100 N. Calculate its mechanical advantage.

MA = Load ÷ Effort

MA = 400 ÷ 100

MA = 4

Example 6: A machine receives 500 J of energy and gives 400 J of useful output energy. Calculate its efficiency.

Efficiency = Useful Output ÷ Total Input × 100%

= 400 ÷ 500 × 100%

= 80%
