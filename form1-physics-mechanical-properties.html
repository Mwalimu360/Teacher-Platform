MECHANICAL PROPERTIES OF MATTER

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of Mechanical Properties

Mechanical properties describe how a material behaves when a force is applied to it. They help us understand whether a material changes shape, stretches, compresses, bends or returns to its original form.

2. Elasticity

Elasticity is the ability of a material to return to its original shape and size after the deforming force is removed, provided the elastic limit has not been exceeded.

Example:
A rubber band can stretch when pulled and return close to its original shape when released.

3. Plasticity

Plasticity is the property of a material that allows it to undergo permanent deformation after the applied force is removed.

Example:
Clay can be permanently shaped by applying a force.

4. Deformation

Deformation is a change in the shape or size of an object caused by an applied force.

5. Elastic Limit

The elastic limit is the maximum extent to which a material can be deformed and still return to its original shape when the force is removed.

6. Hooke's Law

For an elastic material, within its elastic limit, the extension is directly proportional to the applied force.

F ∝ e

where:
F = applied force
e = extension

7. Stiffness

Stiffness is the ability of a material to resist deformation when a force is applied. A stiff material requires a relatively large force to produce a small deformation.

8. Brittleness

Brittleness is the property of a material that causes it to break or fracture with little permanent deformation when a sufficiently large force is applied.

Example:
Glass is a brittle material.

9. Ductility

Ductility is the ability of a material to be drawn into thin wires without breaking.

Example:
Copper is a ductile material.

10. Malleability

Malleability is the ability of a material to be hammered or pressed into thin sheets without breaking.

Example:
Aluminium is a malleable material.

11. Cohesion

Cohesion is the force of attraction between molecules or particles of the same substance.

Example:
The attraction between water molecules is a cohesive force.

12. Adhesion

Adhesion is the force of attraction between molecules or particles of different substances.

Example:
Water molecules are attracted to the surface of glass.

13. Surface Tension

Surface tension is the tendency of the surface of a liquid to behave like a stretched elastic membrane due to cohesive forces between liquid molecules.

14. Capillarity

Capillarity is the rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube due to the combined effects of adhesion and cohesion.

15. Applications in Daily Life

Mechanical properties are important when selecting materials for buildings, bridges, vehicles, electrical wires, containers, clothing and many other objects.

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: What is elasticity?

Elasticity is the ability of a material to return to its original shape and size after the deforming force is removed, provided the elastic limit is not exceeded.

Example 2: State Hooke's Law.

Within the elastic limit, the extension of an elastic material is directly proportional to the applied force.

Example 3: A spring extends by 4 cm when a force of 2 N is applied. What force would produce an extension of 8 cm, assuming Hooke's Law applies?

Since force is directly proportional to extension:

8 cm is twice 4 cm.

Therefore the force is twice 2 N.

F = 4 N

Example 4: Distinguish between cohesion and adhesion.

Cohesion is attraction between particles of the same substance.

Adhesion is attraction between particles of different substances.
