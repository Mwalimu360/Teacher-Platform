MEASUREMENT

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of Measurement

Measurement is the process of comparing an unknown physical quantity with an accepted standard quantity of the same kind.

2. Physical Quantities

A physical quantity is a quantity that can be measured and expressed using a numerical value and a unit. Examples include length, mass, time, temperature and volume.

3. SI Units

The International System of Units (SI) provides standard units used in scientific measurements.

Common SI base quantities and units:

Length → metre (m)
Mass → kilogram (kg)
Time → second (s)
Temperature → kelvin (K)
Electric current → ampere (A)

4. Measuring Length

Length can be measured using instruments such as a ruler, metre rule, measuring tape and vernier calipers depending on the required accuracy.

5. Measuring Mass

Mass is the amount of matter contained in an object. It can be measured using a beam balance or electronic balance. The SI unit of mass is kilogram (kg).

6. Measuring Time

Time can be measured using clocks, stopwatches and other timing devices. The SI unit of time is the second (s).

7. Measuring Temperature

Temperature indicates how hot or cold an object is. It is measured using a thermometer. The SI base unit is kelvin (K), although degrees Celsius (°C) is also commonly used.

8. Measuring Volume

The volume of liquids can be measured using a measuring cylinder. The volume of a regular solid can be calculated from its dimensions.

Volume of a cuboid = length × width × height

9. Accuracy and Precision

Accuracy describes how close a measured value is to the true or accepted value. Precision describes how close repeated measurements are to one another.

10. Measurement Errors

Errors may occur during measurement because of instrument limitations, incorrect reading, parallax or environmental conditions.

11. Parallax Error

Parallax error occurs when a scale is viewed from the wrong position. To reduce it, the eye should be placed directly in line with the reading.

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: Convert 250 centimetres into metres.

100 cm = 1 m

250 cm ÷ 100 = 2.5 m

Answer: 2.5 m

Example 2: Find the volume of a cuboid with length 5 m, width 2 m and height 3 m.

Volume = length × width × height
= 5 × 2 × 3
= 30 m³

Answer: 30 m³

Example 3: A student measures the same object three times and obtains 10.1 cm, 10.1 cm and 10.1 cm. Are the measurements precise?

Yes. The measurements are close to one another, so they are precise.

Example 4: Why should the eye be directly in line with a measurement scale?

To reduce parallax error and obtain a more accurate reading.
