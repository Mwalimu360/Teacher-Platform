SIMPLE MACHINES

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of a Simple Machine

A simple machine is a device that makes work easier by changing the size or direction of an applied force.

Examples: Lever, pulley, wheel and axle, inclined plane, screw and wedge.

2. Importance of Simple Machines

Simple machines are used to make tasks easier by reducing the effort required, changing the direction of force, or increasing the distance through which a force acts.

3. Load and Effort

The load is the resistance or weight that a machine is used to overcome.

The effort is the force applied to a machine to overcome the load.

4. Mechanical Advantage

Mechanical advantage is the ratio of the load to the effort applied to a machine.

Mechanical Advantage (MA)
= Load ÷ Effort

MA = L / E

Mechanical advantage has no SI unit because it is a ratio of two forces.

5. Velocity Ratio

Velocity ratio is the ratio of the distance moved by the effort to the distance moved by the load.

Velocity Ratio (VR)
= Distance moved by effort ÷ Distance moved by load

6. Efficiency of a Machine

Efficiency is the ratio of useful output work to input work, expressed as a percentage.

Efficiency
= Useful Output Work ÷ Input Work × 100%

For a real machine, efficiency is normally less than 100% because some energy is lost, mainly due to friction.

7. Lever

A lever is a rigid bar that can turn about a fixed point called a fulcrum.

Examples: Seesaw, crowbar, wheelbarrow, scissors and bottle opener.

8. Parts of a Lever

  • Fulcrum: the fixed point about which the lever turns.
  • Effort: the force applied to the lever.
  • Load: the resistance overcome by the lever.

9. Classes of Levers

Levers are divided into three classes according to the positions of the fulcrum, load and effort.

First Class Lever

The fulcrum is between the load and the effort.

Examples: Seesaw, scissors and pliers.

Second Class Lever

The load is between the fulcrum and the effort.

Examples: Wheelbarrow, nutcracker and bottle opener.

Third Class Lever

The effort is between the fulcrum and the load.

Examples: Human forearm, tweezers and fishing rod.

10. Pulley

A pulley is a wheel with a groove around its edge over which a rope or belt passes.

Pulleys can be used to change the direction of an effort and, in some arrangements, to reduce the effort needed to lift a load.

11. Fixed Pulley

A fixed pulley is attached to a fixed support. Its main advantage is changing the direction of the effort.

12. Movable Pulley

A movable pulley moves together with the load and can provide mechanical advantage.

13. Inclined Plane

An inclined plane is a sloping surface used to raise or lower objects with less effort than lifting them vertically.

Examples: Ramps used by vehicles, stairs and sloping roads.

14. Wheel and Axle

A wheel and axle consists of a large wheel attached to a smaller cylindrical axle. Turning one causes the other to turn.

Examples: Door handles, steering wheels and some types of winches.

15. Screw

A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder. It can be used for fastening objects or lifting loads.

Examples: Screws, screw jacks and bottle tops.

16. Wedge

A wedge is a simple machine made from one or two inclined planes. It is mainly used for cutting, splitting or holding objects.

Examples: Knife, axe, chisel and door wedge.

17. Friction in Machines

Friction is a force that opposes motion between surfaces in contact. It causes some of the input energy to be converted into heat.

Therefore, friction reduces the efficiency of machines.

18. Summary

Quantity Formula Unit
Mechanical Advantage MA = Load / Effort No unit
Velocity Ratio VR = Distance moved by effort / Distance moved by load No unit
Efficiency Useful output / Input × 100% %

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: A machine lifts a load of 600 N using an effort of 150 N. Calculate its mechanical advantage.

MA = Load ÷ Effort

MA = 600 ÷ 150

MA = 4

Example 2: The effort of a machine moves through 4 m while the load moves through 1 m. Calculate the velocity ratio.

VR = Distance moved by effort ÷ Distance moved by load

VR = 4 ÷ 1

VR = 4

Example 3: A machine receives 500 J of input work and produces 400 J of useful output work. Calculate its efficiency.

Efficiency = Useful Output ÷ Input × 100%

= 400 ÷ 500 × 100%

= 80%

Example 4: A machine has a mechanical advantage of 5 and an effort of 80 N. Find the load.

MA = Load ÷ Effort

Load = MA × Effort

= 5 × 80

Load = 400 N

Example 5: A machine has a load of 900 N and mechanical advantage of 3. Calculate the effort required.

MA = Load ÷ Effort

Effort = Load ÷ MA

= 900 ÷ 3

Effort = 300 N

Example 6: A machine has an input work of 1000 J and an efficiency of 70%. Calculate the useful output work.

Efficiency = Output ÷ Input × 100%

70 = Output ÷ 1000 × 100

Output = 700 J

Useful output work = 700 J
