PRESSURE

Form 1 Physics

📖 Notes

1. Meaning of Pressure

Pressure is the force acting normally per unit area of a surface.

Pressure = Force ÷ Area

P = F / A

2. SI Unit of Pressure

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa). One pascal is equal to one newton per square metre.

1 Pa = 1 N/m²

3. Pressure in Solids

The pressure exerted by a solid depends on the force applied and the area over which the force acts. For the same force, a smaller area produces greater pressure.

4. Applications of Pressure in Solids

Sharp knives, needles, nails and pointed tools have small contact areas so that they produce greater pressure.

5. Pressure in Liquids

Liquid pressure increases with depth. It also depends on the density of the liquid and gravitational acceleration.

P = ρgh

where ρ is the density of the liquid, g is gravitational acceleration and h is the depth.

6. Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the weight of the air surrounding the Earth.

7. Measuring Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure can be measured using a barometer. A mercury barometer is one traditional type of barometer.

8. Applications of Atmospheric Pressure

Atmospheric pressure is involved in the operation of drinking straws, syringes, suction cups and some pumps.

9. Pressure and Depth

As depth below the surface of a liquid increases, the pressure increases. This is why the walls of dams are generally made thicker towards the bottom.

10. Hydraulic Machines

Hydraulic machines use pressure transmitted through a confined liquid. Examples include hydraulic brakes, hydraulic jacks and hydraulic presses.

💡 Worked Examples

Example 1: A force of 100 N acts on an area of 2 m². Calculate the pressure.

P = F ÷ A

P = 100 ÷ 2

P = 50 Pa

Example 2: A force of 200 N acts on an area of 0.5 m². Find the pressure.

P = 200 ÷ 0.5

P = 400 Pa

Example 3: Calculate the pressure due to water at a depth of 2 m. Take density of water as 1000 kg/m³ and g = 10 N/kg.

P = ρgh

P = 1000 × 10 × 2

P = 20,000 Pa

Example 4: Why does a sharp knife cut more easily than a blunt knife?

A sharp knife has a smaller contact area. For the same force, the smaller area produces greater pressure.
